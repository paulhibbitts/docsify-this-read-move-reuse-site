# Why It Matters

<small>Part 1 of 6</small>

> [!TIP]
> **Learning Objectives**
>
> - **Share:** see how one set of course pages works on any device, inside your LMS and beyond it
> - **Move:** see how plain Markdown keeps a course open to adapt or self-host beyond your LMS, on a site you control
> - **Reuse:** get the Markdown behind any page, with no access to the source files needed

**Open beyond the LMS:** course materials often stay inside an LMS – visible only to enrolled students, and harder to reuse next term, share with colleagues or move to another platform. What if every page stayed open and portable?

> [!NOTE]
> **The DIY answer: plain Markdown**
>
> A simple text format for web pages – `**bold**`, `## Heading`, `- list item`. It's supported almost everywhere, from GitHub and note-taking apps to Google Docs. Keep every page in Markdown, on a free site you control.

**Not ready to openly license your work?** Plain Markdown still keeps your course materials yours – ready for next term, a new LMS or a new institution.

Best of all for reuse, you (and, with an open licence, anyone) can get the Markdown behind any page – no source files needed – with its callouts, math and diagrams intact, ready for [Docsify-This](https://docsify-this.net/) (which shows Markdown as web pages) or GitHub.

**See an example:** [an openly licensed LMS course on a course site](https://demo.hibbittsdesign.org/variants/grav-open-course-space-common-cartridge-it2dda/), converted with no revisions – more in [From Your LMS](open-course-hub/from-your-lms.md).

Your LMS still has its place – for grades, quizzes and enrolment. An open course site works alongside it, and its pages embed right inside it.
