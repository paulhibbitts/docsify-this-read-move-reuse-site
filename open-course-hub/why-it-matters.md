# Why It Matters

<small>Part 1 of 6</small>

> [!TIP]
> **Learning Objectives**
>
> - **Portable:** see how plain Markdown keeps a course open to share, adapt or self-host beyond your LMS
> - **Reusable:** get the Markdown behind any page, with no access to the source files needed
> - **Your own:** keep your content, and your learners' data, on a site you control

**Open beyond the LMS:** course materials often stay inside an LMS – visible only to enrolled students, and harder to reuse next term, share with colleagues or move to another platform. What if every page stayed open and portable?

> [!NOTE]
> **The DIY answer: plain Markdown**
>
> A simple text format for web pages – `**bold**`, `## Heading`, `- list item`. It's supported almost everywhere, from GitHub and note-taking apps to Google Docs. Keep every page in Markdown, on a free site you control.

**Not ready to openly license your work?** Plain Markdown still keeps your course materials yours – ready for next term, a new LMS or a new institution.

Best of all for reuse, you (and, with an open licence, anyone) can get the Markdown behind any page – no source files needed – with its callouts, math and diagrams intact, ready for [Docsify-This](https://docsify-this.net/) (which shows Markdown as web pages) or GitHub. That Markdown is also widely used by generative AI tools, so open content is as easy for them to work with as it is for people.

**See an example:** [an openly licensed LMS course in Open Course Hub](https://demo.hibbittsdesign.org/grav-open-course-hub-common-cartridge-it2dda/), converted with no revisions – more in [From Your LMS](open-course-hub/from-your-lms.md).

Your LMS still has its place – for grades, quizzes and enrolment. An open course site works alongside it, and its pages embed right inside it.
